\subsection{Flow Matching Policy vs. MSE Policy}
The flow policy agent takes actions to achieve the goal in a more orderly and consistent way.
In contrast the MSE policy agent either achieves the goal quickly and efficiently or it gets very off track and performs actions that appear not to be leading directly toward pushing the T to the target location. A few hypothesese as to what might be going wrong here: 
\begin{itemize}
\item the MSE policy isn't as equipped to handle multimodal distributions and when in a scenario where it should pick a mode it instead splits the difference leading to a suboptimal action.
\item the MSE policy may be less consistent because its predictions are worse than the flow policy and as a result it gets out of distribution more often.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item Describe in words how $\lambda$ affected task performance.
\begin{itemize}
\item We see that increasing $\lambda$ improves the avg return. In particular, it improves the avg return for the second half of training.
\end{itemize}
\item Consider the parameter $\lambda$. What does $\lambda = 0$ correspond to? What about $\lambda = 1$? Relate this to the task performance in LunarLander-v2 in one or two sentences.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\lambda = 0$ corresponds to $A^{\pi}_{\textbf{GAE}} = \hat{A}^{\pi}(s_t, a_t) = r(s_t, a_t) + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$
\item whereas $\lambda = 1$ corresponds to equal weight being placed on each $A_n^{\pi}(s_t, a_t)$
\item $\lambda = 0$ is high bias low variance, while $\lambda = 1$ is low bias high variance
\item In the graphs we see $\lambda = 0$ has the worst performance while $\lambda = 1$ has the best performance. One hypothesis for why this may be the case is that with $\lambda = 0$ there is too much bias i.e. $V^\pi_\phi$ is not a reliable estimate of the value function, which causes underperformance. Therefore, we benefit from $\lambda = 1$, which gives us lower bias at the cost of higher variance.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
